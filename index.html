How your data is handled

If you're just watching the map

The app never asks for your location on its own, and you can use the whole map without ever giving it. No account, no name, no phone number. We can't see who is watching.

Two things can use your location if you ask them to, and neither one sends it anywhere: the location button on the map, which draws a dot where you are, and “Use my current location” in the stop picker, which finds the nearest stop to you. Both work entirely inside your phone. Your location is never transmitted, never stored on our side, and no other visitor can see it. The dot switches itself off when you close the tab.

If you save a stop, the stop is kept on your device and the distance to the bus is worked out there too — the page already knows where the buses are, so nothing extra is asked of the server and nothing about you is sent to it.

One thing is counted automatically, and it is worth saying plainly: while this page is open it sends a random ID every minute, so the organizer can see how many devices have the map open right now — a single number, nothing more. The ID is made up fresh by this tab, it isn't linked to you, your phone or anything else you do here, and it is deleted three minutes after you close the page. There is no way to go from that number back to a person, and no record of it is kept afterwards.

One more thing can leave your device, and only if you tap it: Salamat, on a bus, to thank whoever is carrying the phone. What's stored is a scrambled value that can't be traced back to you, can't be matched against anything else here, and exists only so a second tap on the same bus isn't counted twice — not your ID, not your location, not which bus you're on. It is deleted the moment that trip ends. Only the person sharing that bus can see the number, and it never adds up across trips: no running total is kept for any driver or any bus, anywhere.

“Send a link to this bus”, in a bus's popup, opens your phone's own sending sheet (or copies the link, where there isn't one) so you can pass it to someone. All the link carries is the bus's public ID, which every visitor's map already has, and it stops working when that trip ends. Nothing about you is in it, and the app sends it nowhere; you do.

If you share from the bus

What's sent: the bus's position, its direction, the bus number if you typed one, and a random ID for your session. Nothing about who you are. Each update overwrites the previous one, so no history of the trip exists anywhere. Not even we can see where a bus has been, only where it is now. When you tap Stop (or close the page), the bus disappears from everyone's map.

In the Android app, sharing keeps going with the screen locked or another app open, and a notification stays up for as long as it does, so it can never run without you knowing. The app sends exactly what this page sends and keeps no more: the latest position, in memory, until you stop.

If riders tap Salamat on your bus, you'll see how many did — for this trip only, and only you. It isn't shown to other riders, it isn't shown to the bus company, and it goes when the trip does. Nothing keeps a total for you, so there is no score to go up or down.

When you tap Stop, you get a ticket for the trip: where you got on and off, how long your bus was on the map, and how many riders said salamat. Your phone makes it from what it saw on the way, and it is not sent anywhere. Close it and it is gone.

Unless you tap Keep: then a copy goes into My tickets, on this phone only. The copy keeps the day, the direction, where you got on and off, the stamps, and a flower if anyone said salamat. It leaves out the times, how long the trip took, the bus number and how many riders thanked you, so a pile of kept tickets cannot time a trip or rank anyone. Nothing is ever kept unless you tap Keep on that ticket, and “Remove all” in My tickets deletes every copy.

To be fully honest

• The map's background images load from CARTO, a map provider. Like any website you visit, they can see your device's IP address. Everything else (code, fonts) is served from this site itself.
• The database runs on Supabase, which keeps standard server logs like any hosting service.
• Anyone who opens this site can see live bus positions, no key needed. That's the point of the tool, but worth saying plainly: while you share, anyone can watch.

Open source

This tool is free and open source under the AGPL-3.0 licence, which means anyone can read it, run it for their own route, and must keep their version open too. See the source code.

Who runs this

A community volunteer, not the bus company. This tool is not affiliated with, run by, or endorsed by Wonderful Transport. The route name and colours are used so you can tell which buses it tracks, nothing more. Positions come from passengers and crew who choose to share, so this is not an official schedule and the company is not responsible for it.

Accuracy is not guaranteed

Positions come from volunteers' phones, not company dispatch or sensors, so a bus can be a few minutes stale, briefly show the wrong direction before the app catches it, or be missing from the map when nobody happens to be sharing. This tool is provided as-is, with no warranty of accuracy or uptime, and is not liable for delays, missed connections, or any other loss from relying on it. If something looks wrong, trust the sightings board, the posted schedule, or your own eyes over the map.

Why you can trust this

This is a free community tool with no ads, nothing to sell, and no analytics beyond the single live count above — no profiles, no cookies, no third-party trackers. There is no business model that needs your data. There is simply nothing collected worth stealing.

Four small things are remembered on your own device, and nowhere else: whether you have opened this guide before (so the dot on the guide button stops nagging), your light or dark theme choice, the stop you saved, if you saved one, and the tickets you chose to keep, if you kept any. All four stay in your browser, are never sent anywhere, and are not linked to anything. Clearing your browser data removes them, “Remove my stop” in the stop picker deletes the third one on its own, and “Remove all” in My tickets the fourth.
