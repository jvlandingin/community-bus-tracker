HOW TO USE THIS TRACKER

Where's the bus?

This page shows where the buses are right now, because someone on board is sharing the position. No app to install, no account, no sign-up. Open the link and look.

Community-run and unofficial. It is not operated by or connected to the bus company.

Watching · no key needed

Reading the map

1

Open the link

Nothing is asked of you. Watching never uses your location and the browser never prompts for it.

2

Read the strip

The strip is the whole route drawn as a line map, Mendez on the left and One Ayala on the right, with a station at every checkpoint. Each round badge is a bus. Gold runs along the top line northbound, maroon along the bottom line southbound. Once you have saved a stop (step 5), it is the purple station on both lines, so you can watch a bus close in on it.

The solid badges are live GPS. The dashed ring is a reported sighting — somebody saw a bus there and said so.

3

Filter by direction

Only care about buses heading to Ayala? Tap ▲ Northbound and the rest drop away.

4

Check how fresh it is

Every bus says when it last moved: updated 2 mins ago. A bus that has gone quiet fades out, and drops off entirely once it is clearly stale. A faded bus is a guess, not a position.

5

Tell it where you wait

Tap Set your stop and pick your usual one from the list. From then on the card under the map does the arithmetic for you. It draws the next bus coming your way at one end of a line and your stop at the other, a dot for every stop still between them, and says it in words: about 5 stops before yours · 3.0 km. That is counted along the road rather than across country, so a bus on the far side of a bend is not reported as nearly here, and it only ever counts buses still coming towards you — one that has already gone past is not an answer.

Two stops out, the card says Malapit na! When yours is the next stop and the bus is close, it says Sakay na! Phones that can vibrate (most Androids, not iPhones) also get a switch to buzz once when it gets that close, so the phone can go in your pocket. The switch is forgotten when you close the page.

If your stop is not on the list, Drop a pin instead and tap the map where you wait. The button on the map shows where you are, if you want it. Your stop stays on this phone. It is not sent anywhere, no other visitor can see it, and “Remove my stop” deletes it.

It tells you distance and stops, never minutes. Working out how long a bus will take needs a record of how long past trips took, and this tool deliberately keeps no such record — see below.

6

Say salamat

Tap a bus — its badge on the strip, its card in the list, or its badge on the map — and its details open, with Say salamat near the bottom. One tap tells whoever is carrying the phone on that bus that somebody was glad it was there, and the bus on your map hops and says beep beep! so you know it landed. They are giving up their battery and data for the rest of us, and until you tap, nothing at all comes back the other way.

Only that one person sees it, only while the trip is running, and it disappears with the trip. There is no score and no total — not for a bus, not for a driver, not anywhere.

7

Send someone the bus

In the same popup, Send a link to this bus opens your phone's own sending sheet with a link that opens the map following that one bus — for whoever is fetching you, or waiting at the other end. On the bus yourself and sharing? Tap your own bus and send it. The link carries nothing about you, only the bus's public ID, which every map already has, and it stops working when the trip ends.

The popup also has a line about the town the bus is passing or heading for. Amadeo, for one, is the coffee capital of the Philippines.

Sightings

When nobody is sharing

Early morning, late evening, or just a quiet stretch: often no one on board is sharing. Post what you saw instead, and everyone else gets something to go on.

Pick the direction and the nearest landmark — you do not type them, so a sighting is always specific enough to be worth reading. Add a note if there is something to add. What you post appears on the route strip as a dashed mark, so the strip still shows something when nobody's GPS does.

A dashed mark is never a live bus, and the strip does not pretend otherwise: it is one person's word, already a few minutes old, with no update coming after it. It clears itself after about one gap between trips.

Sharing · needs the key

I'm on the bus

If you are the driver, the conductor, or a rider willing to help, you can put this bus on everyone's map for the trip.

You need the community key It is posted in the group chat, usually as a link that already carries the key. Open that link and the key is already in.
1

Tap “I'm on the bus”

The second tab, next to the map.

2

Pick the direction

Northbound to One Ayala, southbound to Mendez. Get this right — it decides which rail your bus rides on, and a bus shown going the wrong way is worse for someone waiting than no bus at all.

3

Add the bus number

Optional, but it helps. If someone else on the same bus is also sharing, the number lets the app show one bus instead of two.

4

Start sharing

The browser asks for location permission once. Say yes and the bus appears on the map within seconds. The strip that opens under the button is the one everyone watching is looking at, and your bus is the one with the green ring around it, so you can find yourself on it even when several buses are sharing.

5

Keep the screen on

Sharing only runs while this page is open and the screen is on. Lock the phone or switch apps and the position stops updating. Mounted on the dashboard and plugged in is the way to do it.

While you share, that tab goes dark and the strip glows: easier to read at a glance from a dashboard mount, and on most phones' screens black costs less battery than white. It goes back to normal the moment you stop, or as soon as you switch to the map.

On Android there is also an app, which loads this same page but keeps sharing with the screen locked or while you use another app, with a notification showing for as long as it does. Ask the organizer for it. Watching the map never needs it.

6

Stop when you get off

Tap Stop sharing and the bus leaves everyone's map immediately. Nothing about the trip is kept by the tracker.

What you get instead is a ticket for the trip: a picture of where you got on and where you got off, how long your bus was on everyone's map, how many riders said salamat, and a stamp or two for the shape of the trip — the whole route, before 5 AM or after dark, the first bus on the map. Your phone makes it from what it saw on the way, and it is not sent anywhere. Close it and it is gone.

Tap Keep and a copy goes into My tickets, on the sharing tab, on this phone only. Every kept ticket has its own picture, and the album counts the places on the route your trips have passed and the stamps you have collected. A kept copy keeps the day, the places, the stamps and a flower if anyone said salamat. It leaves out the times, how long the trip took, the bus number and the count, so nobody can use a pile of them to time a trip or rank a driver.

Your data

What this never keeps

No accounts, no names, no phone numbers. No location history at all — there is one row per active share, overwritten in place, and it is deleted the moment you stop. There is no trail to look back through, so nobody can review a driver's speed, breaks, or route. The data to do that does not exist.

The one thing that can be kept is yours. Tap Keep on a ticket and a copy stays in My tickets, on your phone and never sent anywhere: the day, the places and the stamps, with no times, no bus number and no count.

Nothing about the people watching, either. The map never needs your location, and the two places that can use it — the button and “Use my current location” in the stop picker — work entirely inside your phone. A saved stop is kept on your own device and never sent anywhere, so no part of this system can see where anybody is waiting.

The full explanation lives in “How your data is handled” on the map page.
